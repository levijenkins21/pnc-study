Key terms — umbrella vs. excess

Underlying Policy (Primary)
The policy that responds first. Umbrella/excess kicks in after primary limits are exhausted. Example: homeowners + auto + watercraft policies are all underlying for a personal umbrella.
Umbrella — Stand-Alone
Coverage can be greater or less than the underlying policies. Can cover losses the primary doesn't cover (triggers SIR). Broader than excess.
Excess — Follow Form
Does not expand or restrict coverage — mirrors the underlying policy exactly. Only function: increases the limit of liability based on amounts the insured selects.

Personal umbrella — overview & SIR

Also called catastrophe insurance. Provides coverage over the underlying policy.

UMBRELLA
Kicks in after primary is exhausted. Minimum $1,000,000 in additional coverage.
PRIMARY POLICY
Homeowners, auto, watercraft. Pays first. No deductible to access umbrella for covered losses.
SIR
Self-Insured Retention — acts as a deductible only when the umbrella covers a loss the primary policy does NOT. The insured pays SIR before umbrella responds.
  • Insured must maintain minimum limits on all underlying policies
  • If underlying coverage lapses and isn't replaced, insurer must be notified immediately
  • If underlying isn't maintained → umbrella only pays what it would have paid if underlying was in effect

Personal umbrella — who is an insured

Named insured
Family member
Any person using an auto or recreational vehicle owned by the insured and covered under the policy — including ATV, dune buggy, golf cart, snowmobile, or off-road motorized vehicle. Also includes users of a temporary substitute.
Any person or organization legally responsible for acts of the insured or family member while using an auto or recreational vehicle. Exception: the owner or lessor of a loaned/hired vehicle is NOT an insured.
Any person or organization legally responsible for animals owned by the insured or family member. Exception: person using or having custody of animals in the course of a business or without the owner's consent is NOT an insured.

Personal umbrella — exclusions

Intentional injury or damage
Personal injury from knowing publication of false material
BI/PD from ownership, maintenance, use, loading/unloading of recreational vehicle owned by any insured
Motor vehicle use without reasonable belief of entitlement to do so
Motor vehicle or watercraft used in a prearranged race or speed contest
Acts or omissions as an officer or Board of Directors member of a corporation
UM/UIM
Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist coverage is excluded from the umbrella — but can be reinstated by endorsement in states where it is required.

Umbrella — defense & settlement

When Underlying Coverage IS in Effect
Umbrella insurer may join in investigation and settlement — but will not contribute to expenses incurred by the underlying insurer. Primary insurer handles defense costs.
When Underlying Coverage is NOT in Effect
Umbrella insurer provides defense at the insurer's expense, using counsel of the insurer's choice.
Consent to Settle — KEY DIFFERENCE from Professional Liability
The umbrella insurer may settle any claim WITHOUT the consent of the insured. This is the opposite of professional liability, where insured consent is required.
Recreational Vehicles
Umbrella follows the underlying policies' coverage for watercraft and recreational vehicles. Optional endorsements can exclude designated recreational vehicles.

Commercial umbrella

  • Provides excess protection over general liability, business auto liability, and many other liability programs
  • Usually requires minimum $1,000,000 on underlying policies
  • SIR applies for losses not covered by any underlying insurance
How it flows
UMBRELLA
Pays after primary exhausted. For uncovered losses: pays after insured satisfies SIR.
↑
PRIMARY
Commercial GL, business auto, etc. Pays first up to its limits.
Commercial Umbrella
Same distinction as personal — can differ from underlying, covers gaps, triggers SIR for uncovered losses
Commercial Excess
Follow-form — mirrors underlying coverage, higher limits only. SIR applies after insured pays when no primary coverage
