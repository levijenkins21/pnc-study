Casualty & Liability Insurance

Coverage types, key terms, and who each policy is designed for

What you'll learn in chapter 4

  • Commercial General Liability (CGL) — broad business liability coverage
  • Workers Compensation — employer obligations when employees are injured
  • Personal & Business Auto — coverage for private and commercial vehicles
  • Professional Liability — coverage for errors and omissions in professional services

By the end, you'll be able to explain each coverage's features, benefits, and who it's right for.

Key terms — valuation & coverage scope

Actual cash value
A valuation method that recognizes property loses value over time due to age and wear and tear. You get what it was worth at the time of loss — not replacement cost.
All risk / Open peril
A policy form that covers any risk of loss not specifically excluded. Broader than named-peril. If the policy doesn't exclude it, it's covered.
Aggregate
The maximum total the insurer pays across all claims during a policy period — not per claim, but for the full policy year.

Key terms — parties & responsibility

Bailee
Someone entrusted with possession of another person's property but with no ownership interest. Example: a service station holding your car for repairs.
Financial responsibility
Proof you can pay for damages you cause — most commonly fulfilled by purchasing insurance. Required by law in most states.
Lessor
A person granting a lease of a motor vehicle. The lessor owns it; the lessee uses it.

Key terms — policy mechanics

Inception
The date the insurance policy goes into effect. Coverage begins at inception.
Insolvent
Unable to meet financial obligations. An insolvent insurer can't pay claims — relevant when evaluating carrier reliability.
Statute
A written law passed by a legislative body. Many insurance requirements (like Workers Comp) are statutory — mandated by law, not optional.

Key terms — auto & umbrella

Private passenger vehicle
An automobile used for private personal needs — not business use. This distinction matters because personal and commercial auto policies are separate products.
SIR — self-insured retention
A deductible paid by the insured to access an umbrella policy when underlying coverage won't cover the loss. You absorb the SIR amount first; then the umbrella kicks in.
