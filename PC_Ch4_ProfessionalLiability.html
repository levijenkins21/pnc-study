Professional Liability

E&O · Medical Malpractice · D&O · EPLI · Cyber Liability · Funds Transfer Fraud · Liquor Liability

Professional liability — overview

  • Covers professionals who provide services for a fee
  • Protects against legal liability from negligence, errors and omissions, and rendering (or failure to render) professional services
  • Most policies written on a claims-made basis
Types of Professionals Covered
Physicians, surgeons, dentists — malpractice for errors in rendering or failing to render services
Lawyers — financial advice, contracts, legal services; wide range of E&O exposure
Insurance agents & brokers — incorrect advice, coverage not placed, failure to inform
Consent to Settle
Insurer cannot settle a claim without the insured's consent. If the insured disputes negligence, they can require the insurer to defend in court — even if settlement would be cheaper. Reason: the professional's reputation is at stake.

Professional liability vs. general/personal liability

General / Personal Liability
Covers losses caused by negligence of the insured. Excludes intentional acts.
Professional Liability
Also covers some intentional acts in addition to negligence. Example: surgeon amputates the wrong leg — intended to amputate, but it was the wrong one due to misdiagnosis. Criminal acts still excluded.
Settling a Claim
Insurer decides whether to defend or pay — usually chooses the least expensive option.
Settling a Claim
Insurer cannot settle without insured's consent. Insured can force court defense to protect professional reputation.
Coverage Trigger
Usually occurrence-based
Coverage Trigger
Nearly always claims-made
Key phrase for exam
"Professional liability" now replaces "malpractice insurance" and "errors and omissions insurance" as the umbrella term for all professional coverage fields.

E&O and medical malpractice

Errors & Omissions (E&O)
Protects insurance agents and brokers from financial losses when insureds sue to recover losses caused by:
  • Giving incorrect advice
  • Not placing requested coverage
  • Not informing the insured of important issues
Written on a claims-made basis. States vary on interpretation. Coverage is very similar to professional liability.
Medical Malpractice
Written for doctors, hospitals, and medical practitioners to indemnify for injuries to third parties due to legal liability for bodily injury or death.
  • Responds to claims of dereliction of professional duty, failure of professional skill, misconduct, negligence, or incompetence
  • Covers some intentional acts (wrong surgery, wrong tooth extraction) — but not criminal acts
  • Nearly all written on claims-made basis

Directors & Officers (D&O) liability

What it covers
Protection for directors and officers (past and present) of an organization for claims arising from wrongful acts committed while acting in an official capacity.
Coverage Trigger
Wrongful acts — not accident or occurrence.
Includes: misstatements, neglect, breach of duty
What D&O Will NOT Pay
Fines, penalties, or punitive damages
Key distinction
D&O pays only damages the corporation would be legally required to reimburse the director or officer. It's essentially corporate indemnification coverage.
  • Covers past and present directors and officers
  • Claim must arise from acting in an official capacity
  • Trigger is wrongful act, not accident or occurrence

Employment Practices Liability (EPLI)

Critical gap to know
EPLI claims are NOT covered by CGL or the employers liability section of WC. EPLI fills this gap.
What EPLI Covers — Employer's Capacity Claims
Refusal to employ, termination, demotion, failure to promote, negative evaluation, reassignment, discipline, defamation or humiliation based on discrimination
Work-related sexual harassment
Other work-related verbal, physical, mental, or emotional abuse related to race, color, national origin, gender, marital status, age, sexual orientation, physical or mental condition, or any other protected class established by federal, state, or local law

Funds transfer fraud

Funds Transfer Fraud Coverage
Covers loss of funds from fraudulent instructions received by a financial institution to pay money from the insured's transfer account to someone else. Computer fraud is excluded from this coverage.
Computer Systems Rider
Protects against fraudulent transfer of funds initiated through the financial institution's computer system. A customer, dishonest employee, or hacker can be responsible. Access may be via PC, internet, or home banking. Covers damage and reconstruction from virus or hacker activity.
3 Insuring Agreements — Electronic Transaction Losses
1
Fraudulent Entry of Electronic Data / Computer Programs
Fraudulent transfer of funds via electronic means
2
Destruction of Data / Computer Programs
Damage from virus, hacker, or employee sabotage
3
Toll Fraud
Long-distance charges from fraudulent use of accounts or passwords

Liquor liability (dram shop)

Also Known As
Dram shop liability — named after laws that hold establishments responsible for serving alcohol
Who Needs It
Bars, restaurants, and establishments involved in selling, distributing, manufacturing, or serving alcoholic beverages. Recall that the CGL and BOP exclude liquor liability for these businesses.
What it covers
Protection when action is brought against the insured for selling liquor to a customer who is later involved in an accident — covering bodily injury or property damage resulting from that accident.
  • Exposure exists under state or local dram shop statutes that establish responsibility for injuries from distribution or use of alcohol
  • Covers injuries to the user or injuries to others caused by the user
  • Businesses: manufacturing · distributing · selling · serving · furnishing alcoholic beverages
