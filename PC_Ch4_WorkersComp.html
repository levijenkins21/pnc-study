Historical — common law defenses

Before WC laws, employees had to prove employer negligence to recover. Even then, employers used these defenses to avoid paying:

Fellow Servant Rule
If the injury was caused by a fellow employee's negligence, it was not considered the employer's negligence.
Contributory Negligence
If the injured employee was even partially responsible for the injury, they were completely barred from recovering damages.
Assumption of Risk
Employee knew the risks of the job in advance and had been compensated for those risks through their salary.
States began diluting these defenses with employer's liability laws → by early 1900s, compulsory WC laws established absolute liability — compensation required regardless of fault.

Federal compensation programs

OSHA — 1970
Set minimum work safety standards. States can retain jurisdiction if their standards meet or exceed OSHA minimums.
FECA
Federal Employees Compensation Act — covers federal civil employees
LHWCA
US Longshore & Harbor Workers Act — crews of vessels, employees in shipbuilding or repair
DBA
Defense Base Act — civilians employed on a military base
Black Lung
Federal Black Lung Compensation — coal miners
FELA
1908
Federal Employers Liability Act — railroad employees. Exempt from many state WC laws. Allows workers to sue employer for negligence — removes contributory negligence and assumption of risk defenses.

Who is an employee & employer

Employee Definition
Person hired to perform a service under the control of the employer, paid a wage or salary, doing work usual and customary to the business. Includes full-time, part-time, seasonal, temporary, leased, borrowed, and unpaid volunteers.

Independent contractors are NOT employees (unless classification is for tax purposes only).
5 Factors to Determine Employee Status
Employer controls the person's task
Work is consistent with the nature of the business
Person is paid a wage or compensation
Business has right to hire or fire
Business furnishes materials for the person to do their work
Farm workers and domestic servants are exempt from WC in most states.

WC policy general section — 5 parts

1. Policy
Summarizes all components. Information page substitutes for the Declarations page. Terms can only be changed by endorsement.
2. Who is Insured
Employer named on information page. For partnerships, each partner is insured only as an employer of the partnership's employees.
3. WC Law
Policy refers to the WC laws in states listed in 3A (states where employer has employees). No dollar amounts shown — policy pays whatever the state's statute requires.
4. State
Defined as any US state + District of Columbia.
5. Locations
Coverage applies to all locations listed in the information page and in 3A, unless those states have other insurance or self-insurance in place.
Key Point
Medical payments under WC are unlimited — no dollar or time limits for necessary medical and surgical expenses (certain types of care may have limits).

WC benefits

🏥
Medical — unlimited payments for medical and surgical expenses. No dollar or time limits (except possibly certain care types).
💵
Disability Income — % of lost wages after a waiting period. Retroactive to day 1 if disability exceeds the waiting period. Subject to weekly max/min.
🎓
Rehabilitation — vocational training, physical/mental therapy, board, lodging, travel. States may impose maximums.
⚰️
Death Benefits — small amount for funeral/burial + weekly income to surviving spouse/children. Continues for life of spouse or until remarriage.
🔄
Second Injury Fund — covers additional benefits when an employee with a prior disability suffers a second injury that worsens the total disability. Encourages employers to hire disabled workers.

Types of disability benefits

Temporary Total Disability
Total inability to work for a limited time. Benefits = % of wages, subject to weekly max/min.
Permanent Total Disability
Total inability to work permanently. Benefits = % of wages, subject to weekly max/min.
Temporary Partial Disability
Ability to do some or alternative work. Benefits = % of the difference between pre- and post-injury wages.
Permanent Partial (Scheduled)
Schedule of benefits for specific losses (limb, hearing, eyesight). Usually paid in addition to other benefits.
Generally Excluded from WC
Injuries traveling to/from work
Injuries from intoxication
Injuries willfully caused by the employee
Willful failure to follow safety precautions
Activities not part of the job

Other states insurance — 3A vs. 3C

3A
States where employer currently has employees — mandatory WC coverage. No dollar limits stated; policy pays whatever that state's law requires.
3C
Other states (excluding monopolistic states) — covers new or incidental operations automatically and temporarily. Most insurers list all non-monopolistic states here for maximum protection.
How 3C Coverage Works
  • New work begins after policy effective date → automatic coverage as if listed in 3A
  • Work is already underway on effective date → insurer must be notified within 30 days or no coverage
  • In subsequent policy periods, the state must be added to 3A by January 30 (30 days into new term) or coverage lapses
Example
Work begins in June in a new state listed in 3C → covered. In year 2, work continues. Injury occurs in March. Insurer must have been notified by end of January — if not, no coverage even if still in 3C.

Employers liability — limits & trigger

Unlike WC (no stated limits), Employers Liability limits are shown on the information page:

$100,000
Bodily injury per accident
$100,000
Bodily injury by disease per employee
$500,000
Bodily injury by disease policy limit (all disease claims during policy term)
Coverage Triggered When ALL 4 Conditions Met
Injury arises from employment by the insured
Occurs in a state listed in 3A
Occurs during the policy period
Suit brought in US, territories, possessions, or Canada

Employers liability — exclusions

Liability assumed under a contract
Punitive or exemplary damages
Employees knowingly hired in violation of law
Injury intentionally caused by the insured
Injuries outside the US, territories, or Canada — exception: residents temporarily outside are covered
Employment practices — defamation, harassment, discrimination, termination
Employees subject to federal WC or employers liability laws (coverage usually available by endorsement)
Fines or penalties for violation of state or federal laws
Damages under Migrant & Seasonal Agricultural Worker Protection Act or similar laws
Other insurance: losses paid on contribution by equal shares basis.

Exclusive remedy & employers liability gaps

Exclusive Remedy Doctrine
Under compulsory WC laws, injured employees are barred from suing the employer outside of WC — WC is the only remedy.
Situations Where Employers Liability Fills the Gap
⚖️
Exempt or illegal employment — workers not covered by WC statute
🔄
Third-party over claims — employee sues a third party → third party sues employer back
🏭
Dual capacity — employee and employer in two separate relationships (e.g., injured by a product the employer manufactures). Employee can collect WC and additional compensation.
👨‍👩‍👧
Loss of consortium / consequential BI — family member claims for loss of companionship or injury resulting from employee's injury
🏢
Parent-subsidiary claims — employee injured by subsidiary, seeks additional compensation from parent company

Premium determination

Basic Rating Formula
Rate per $100 of payroll × job classification rate (set by rating bureau, e.g. NCCI).
Payroll = salaries, wages, commissions, bonuses, noncash compensation, vacation, sick leave.
Experience Modification Factor (Mod)
Developed by a rating bureau. Compares employer's actual losses and payroll against expected. Below 1.0 = credit (discount), above 1.0 = debit (surcharge).
Premium Discount
Applies when total standard premium exceeds $5,000. Recognizes economies of scale for larger accounts.
Participating Policies (Safety Groups)
Eligible for dividends (partial premium refund) if loss experience meets guidelines. Dividends are not guaranteed. No penalty for high losses.
Retrospective Rating
For large commercial accounts. Final premium based on actual loss experience during policy term. Subject to minimum and maximum premium. Formula guaranteed in contract.
Estimated payroll used at policy issuance → final premium determined by audit.

Compulsory vs. elective WC laws

Compulsory (most states)
All employers must provide WC coverage (except those excluded by staff size or employment type). Exclusive remedy — employees cannot sue outside WC.
Elective (few states)
Employer can opt out of state WC laws. However, if they opt out, they lose common law defenses against liability suits.
NCCI Standard Policy
  • All WC & Employers Liability policies based on NCCI (National Council on Compensation Insurance) standard policy
  • May contain slight variations by state or insurer
  • Written through private insurers, or state funds in monopolistic states
Work-Related Requirement
  • Must arise out of or during employment
  • Occupational disease requires a direct relationship between job and disease — ordinary public diseases not covered
  • Penalties for employer's willful failure to provide safety equipment — NOT covered by WC insurance, paid by employer
