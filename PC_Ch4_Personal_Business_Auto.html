Personal Auto Policy (PAP) — overview

Combines physical damage and liability for private passenger vehicle owners. Covers 3 types of potential loss:

Loss Type 1
Legal liability for injuries/property damage to others
Loss Type 2
Injury to the insured or family members
Loss Type 3
Damage to or loss of the auto itself

Key definitions

Bodily injury
Bodily harm, sickness or disease, including death that results from the injury.
Property damage
Physical injury, destruction, or loss of use of tangible property.
"You" and "Your"
The named insured on the Declarations + their spouse if resident of same household. Departing spouse retains coverage for the earliest of: 90 days after leaving, effective date of their new policy, or end of policy period.
Family member
Person related by blood, marriage, or adoption who is a resident of the household — including ward or foster children.
Occupying
In, upon, getting in, on, out of, or off a vehicle.

Your covered auto

  • Any vehicle shown in the declarations
  • A newly acquired auto
  • Any trailer owned by the insured
  • Any auto/trailer not owned but used as a temporary substitute while covered vehicle is out of use
Newly Acquired Auto — Notification Deadlines
Liability, Med Pay, UM/UIM
14 days to notify insurer
Collision / OTC — if already on policy
14 days — broadest coverage applies
Collision / OTC — NOT already on policy
4 days to notify. If loss occurred before notifying: $500 deductible applies.

Types of autos

Owned
Eligible vehicles titled by the insured or acquired during the policy period.
Nonowned
Auto operated by or in custody of insured/family member, not titled to or furnished for regular use of the insured.
Hired
Leased, hired, rented, or borrowed — excluding autos owned by employees.
Temporary Substitute
Non-owned auto used while a covered auto is out for breakdown, repair, loss, or destruction.
Hired vs. Nonowned: The key distinction is ownership by an employee — autos leased/hired/rented from employees are nonowned, not hired. Payment does not determine the classification.

Collision vs. other-than-collision

Collision
Upset or impact of a covered vehicle with another vehicle or object.
Other-Than-Collision (OTC)
  • Missiles or falling objects
  • Fire / Explosion or earthquake
  • Theft or larceny
  • Windstorm / Hail, water, or flood
  • Vandalism or malicious mischief
  • Riot or civil commotion
  • Contact with birds or animals
  • Breakage of glass
OTC was previously called comprehensive. It is a property insurance coverage.

Part A — liability coverage

Protects insured from legal liability when a covered auto is involved in an accident. Covers:

  • Bodily injury and property damage the insured is legally responsible for
  • Defense costs — paid in addition to policy limits (as long as coverage applies)
  • Costs for settling or defending — end when limits are exhausted
Who is an insured — Part A
  • Insured or any family member using any auto or trailer
  • Any person using a covered auto with permission
  • Any organization liable for the insured's acts or omissions
  • Any person/org liable for family member's acts (if they don't own/hire the auto)
Supplementary payments (paid in addition to limits): Up to $250 bail bonds · $200/day loss of earnings · Appeal bond premiums · Accrued interest on judgment

Part B — medical payments coverage

  • Pays reasonable medical and funeral expenses caused by an accident
  • Services must be rendered within 3 years of the accident
  • Applies per person, regardless of fault
  • Works similar to Personal Injury Protection (PIP)
  • Payments available under liability or UM will reduce the amount payable under Part B
Who is an insured — Part B
  • Named insured or family member occupying any motor vehicle designed for public road use
  • Named insured or family member as a pedestrian struck by a motor vehicle
  • Any other person while occupying a covered auto
Example
$10,000 Med Pay limit. Two people injured: one with $12K in injuries, one with $10K. Part B pays $10,000 each — up to the per-person limit.

Part C — uninsured & underinsured motorists

4 Categories of Uninsured Motor Vehicles
1. No liability bond or insurance at all
2. Coverage is less than the state's financial responsibility law requires
3. Hit-and-run — driver cannot be identified
4. Insurer denies coverage or is insolvent
Underinsured Motorist (UIM) Formula
Total Damage – At-Fault Driver's Limit = UIM Pays
(up to your UIM policy limit)
Example: Bob hits Zoe. $65K damage. Bob's limit = $25K.
Gap = $40K. If Zoe has 100/150 UIM → covered fully. If Zoe has 30/50 UIM → pays $30K, leaving $10K uncovered.

Part D — coverage for damage to your auto

  • Covers direct and accidental loss to a covered or nonowned auto
  • Minus any applicable deductible shown in declarations
  • Multiple covered autos in same collision → only highest deductible applies
  • Nonowned trailers limited to $1,500
  • Permanently installed electronic equipment covered up to $1,000
Limit of Liability — Lesser of:
Actual cash value at time of loss (adjusted for depreciation)
Amount necessary to repair or replace the vehicle
Transportation Expenses
  • $20/day up to $600 for covered loss
  • Begins 24 hours after loss (non-theft) / 48 hours after theft
  • Not subject to deductible

Key exclusions — Part A (liability)

Intentional injury or damage
Property owned or being transported by insured
Vehicles used as public/livery conveyance (not carpools/charity)
Driver logged into TNP (rideshare platform)
Vehicle used without permission
Vehicles with fewer than 4 wheels or designed for off-road
Vehicles at racing facility for racing or speed contest
Bodily injury to employee during employment (except domestic workers)
TNP = Transportation Network Platform (e.g., rideshare apps)

Out-of-state coverage & DOC endorsement

Out-of-State Coverage
  • If another state's financial responsibility law requires higher limits → policy provides those higher limits automatically
  • If state requires nonresident insurance minimums → policy adjusts to meet them
  • No duplicate payments for the same loss
Drive Other Car (DOC) Endorsement
Individual Named Insured
Extends liability + physical damage to nonowned vehicles driven by the insured
Drive Other Car (Commercial)
Added to a commercial auto policy to cover employees (usually executives) who drive nonowned/rented vehicles when they lack a personal auto policy

Garagekeepers coverage — Section III

Covers damage to customer's autos left in the insured's care, custody, and control for service, repairs, parking, or storage. Triggered by Symbol 30.

Coverage Options
Legal Liability (Standard)
Triggered only when insured is legally liable for the damage — default option.
Direct Coverage Option
Triggered regardless of liability — must be activated on declarations + extra premium.
Excess Option
Pays on excess basis over customer's own insurance, without regard to insured's legal liability.
Primary Option
Pays as primary insurer regardless of other insurance available to pay for the loss.
Causes of Loss Covered
Comprehensive (all except collision/overturn)
Specified causes (fire, theft, vandalism, etc.)
Collision (impact or overturn)
