Key definitions — Part 1

Occurrence
An accident, including continuous or repeated exposure to the same harmful conditions. Covers events during the policy period and after if arising from the same circumstances.
Bodily Injury
Bodily injury, sickness, or disease — including resulting death — sustained by a person.
Property Damage
Physical injury to tangible property including loss of use. Also includes loss of use of property not physically injured. Electronic data is NOT tangible property.
Coverage Territory
  • USA, territories, possessions, Puerto Rico, and Canada
  • International waters/airspace during travel to/from defined territories
  • Anywhere in the world if injury results from goods manufactured or sold in a coverage territory

Key definitions — Part 2

Personal and Advertising Injury
Injury (including consequential BI) from: false arrest/detention/imprisonment · wrongful eviction or invasion of privacy · malicious prosecution · slander or libel · copyright/trade secret/slogan infringement in an ad · use of another's advertising idea · publication violating another's privacy.
Employee
Includes a leased worker (placed by a labor leasing firm). Does NOT include a temporary worker (furnished to substitute or meet short-term needs).
Mobile Equipment
Bulldozers, farm machinery, forklifts, crawler-tread vehicles, vehicles used to power cranes/shovels/drills, road construction/resurfacing equipment. Exception: if subject to compulsory insurance law → not mobile equipment.
Auto
Land motor vehicle, trailer, or semitrailer designed for public roads including attached machinery. Also includes any land vehicle requiring compulsory insurance where licensed or garaged.

Coverage A — bodily injury & property damage

Insuring Agreement
Insurer pays sums the insured is legally obligated to pay as damages for BI or PD within the coverage territory. Also has the right and duty to defend the insured in any covered suit.
  • Defense ends when limits of insurance are exhausted
  • Insurer may investigate any occurrence at its own discretion
  • Pays no more than policy limits regardless of claims
  • Covers premises, operations, products, and completed operations
Key concept
CGL coverage is not determined by perils or causes of loss — it's determined by exclusions. If no exclusion applies, the policy responds.

Coverage A — exclusions (1 of 2)

Expected / Intentional
No coverage for intentional injury — except reasonable force to protect persons/property.
Contractual Liability
No coverage for assumed liability via contract — unless the insured would've been liable anyway, or it's an insured contract (leases, sidetrack agreements, municipal indemnification, elevator maintenance).
Liquor Liability
Excluded if insured is in the business of manufacturing, selling, or serving alcohol. Social hosts are NOT excluded.
Workers Comp / Employers Liability
No BI coverage for employees injured during employment — covered under WC policy.
Pollution
Catastrophic exposure — excluded for nearly all causes.
War
Excluded whether declared or not.

Coverage A — exclusions (2 of 2)

Aircraft / Auto / Watercraft
Excluded — except: nonowned watercraft <26 ft; watercraft ashore on insured's premises; parking a nonowned auto on insured's property; liability assumed via insured contract; mobile equipment operation.
Mobile Equipment
No coverage for transportation by an insured's auto, or while used in a race or contest.
Damage to Property
No coverage for property owned, rented, loaned to, or in care/custody of the insured.
Insured's Product / Work
No coverage for damage to the insured's own product or work — exception: work done by a subcontractor is covered.
Impaired Property
No coverage for PD to impaired property caused by defect in insured's product/work or failure to perform per contract.
Electronic Data
No coverage for loss of, damage to, corruption of, or inability to access electronic data.

Coverage B — personal & advertising injury

What it covers
Specific acts causing non-physical injury to third parties: false arrest · wrongful eviction · malicious prosecution · slander/libel · copyright or advertising idea infringement · invasion of privacy. Offense must occur within coverage territory and policy period.
Key Exclusions — Coverage B
Knowing violation of another's rights — intentional acts
Material published before policy period
Known falsity of published material
Criminal acts by insured or at insured's direction
Infringement of copyright, patent, trademark, trade secret (not advertising idea)
Insureds in media, broadcasting, or Internet businesses
Wrong price in advertising material
Electronic chatrooms/bulletin boards owned by insured

Coverage C — medical payments

What it covers
Necessary medical, surgical, ambulance, hospital, professional nursing, and funeral expenses for third parties. Paid without regard to fault — this is "goodwill" coverage.
Key Rule
Expenses must be incurred and reported within 1 year (12 months) of the date of the accident.
Exclusions — Coverage C
Any insured (except volunteer workers)
Employees or anyone hired by the insured
Tenants of the insured
Benefits required under workers comp law
Persons injured in sports/athletic contests
War, insurrection, rebellion, or revolution

Coverage summary — A, B, C

Coverage A — Bodily Injury & Property Damage
BI: injury, sickness, disease, or death of third parties from business activities.
PD: injury to, destruction of, or loss of use of others' property.
Coverage B — Personal & Advertising Injury
PI: mental anguish, false arrest, wrongful eviction, malicious prosecution, defamation, slander/libel, invasion of privacy.
AI: inadvertent libel, slander, defamation, or privacy violation in advertising.
Coverage C — Medical Payments
Medical, surgical, ambulance, hospital, nursing, funeral expenses for third parties — no fault required. Reported within 12 months of accident.
Supplementary Payments — Coverages A & B
Paid in addition to limits: insurer expenses · bail bonds up to $250 · loss of income up to $250/day · court costs · prejudgment and accrued interest.

Section II — who is an insured

Individual (Sole Proprietor)
Named insured + spouse — for business conduct only
Partnership / Joint Venture
Named insured, members, partners + spouses — for business conduct
LLC
Named insured + members for business. Managers insured for duties as managers.
Other Organizations
Named insured + executive officers, directors, stockholders for their duties
Trust
Named insured + trustees for duties as trustees
Newly Acquired Organizations
Auto-covered for up to 90 days after acquisition (or policy expiration, whichever is earlier). Not partnerships, JVs, or LLCs.
Also insureds regardless of entity: employees/volunteers (scope of employment) · real estate agents · temporary custodians of property upon insured's death.

Section III — limits of insurance

Typical CGL Declarations page:

CoverageLimit
Each Occurrence Limit$1,000,000
↳ Damage to Property Rented to You$100,000
↳ Medical Expense Limit$10,000
Personal & Advertising Injury Limit$1,000,000
General Aggregate$2,000,000
Products/Completed Ops Aggregate$2,000,000
How limits work
  • Limits apply regardless of number of insureds, claimants, or suits
  • Products/Completed Ops draws down a separate aggregate
  • Medical payments are per person but subject to occurrence limit
  • Once general aggregate depletes, all coverage ends, including supplementary payments
  • Aggregate limits restore at policy anniversary

Section IV — key conditions

Bankruptcy — does not relieve the insurer of its obligations
Insured's Duties — notify insurer ASAP; send demands/summons immediately; authorize records; no voluntary payments without consent (at insured's own cost)
Legal Action vs. Insurer — no party can join the insurer as a party in a suit; must fulfill all policy provisions before suing the insurer
Other Insurance — pays on a primary basis (shared equally or pro rata), or excess basis when insured is added as additional insured on another policy
Premium Audit — insurer may audit books/records at end of policy term
Separation of Insureds — coverage applies to each insured separately; does not increase limits
Subrogation — insured must transfer recovery rights to insurer; cannot impede those rights after a loss
Nonrenewal — typically 30 days notice (may vary by state)

Occurrence vs. claims-made

Occurrence Form
Most common. Coverage triggered when the event occurs — even if claim is made years later.

Policy in force at time of injury responds.
Claims-Made Form
Coverage triggered when the claim is first made. Used for professional liability, products, employment, and environmental.

Policy in force when claim is reported responds.
Example — Martin & Sandy (brownie business)
Occurrence: Injury in 2020, suit filed in 2022 → 2020 policy responds.
Claims-Made (retro date 1/1/2018): Same scenario → 2022 policy responds (injury after retro date). If injury was in 2017 → no coverage (before retro date).
Claims-made used when occurrences can manifest slowly or span many years.

Claims-made unique features

Retroactive Date
Entered on declarations. Any claim arising from an incident before this date is not covered — no matter when the claim is made.
Extended Reporting Period (ERP) — "Tail Coverage"
Allows the insured to report a claim after policy expiration or cancellation. Claim must still have occurred on or after the retroactive date.

Think of it as: last policy period = HEAD · ERP = TAIL
Why it matters
Example: Attorney retires, cancels policy. ERP allows claims to still be reported against the last policy in force — even though coverage is no longer active.
Claim Information on Claims-Made
Quantitative — amounts claimed are known (filed claims)
Nonquantitative — occurrences reported but claims not yet filed; future amounts unknown

Extended reporting periods (ERP)

Basic ERP — No Additional Premium
MINI TAIL
Unknown claim — only 60 days after policy expiration. If notice received after day 60 → no coverage.
MIDI TAIL
Known claim — reported within 60 days of expiration → extended coverage for up to 5 years after policy expiration.
Supplemental ERP — Must Be Purchased by Endorsement
  • Provides unlimited time to report a claim
  • Must elect within 60 days of policy expiration
  • Does not take effect until premium is paid
  • Insurer can charge up to 200% of annual premium
  • Excess over any other valid insurance
  • Resets both aggregate limits (General and Products/Completed Ops) to declarations amounts

Exposures & damage to property limits

Premises & Operations
Ownership, maintenance, or use of the insured's premises and all business operations. All liability from either is covered (subject to exclusions).
Products & Completed Operations
BI or PD occurring away from the insured's premises after the job is done or the product is sold. Coverage is optional (included for some risks).
Limits Structure
  • Per Occurrence Limit — maximum per accident regardless of number of claimants
  • General Aggregate — maximum for ALL losses (Cov A, B, C) during policy period
  • Products/Completed Ops Aggregate — separate from general aggregate
  • Fire Damage Legal Liability — specific limit for fire damage to rented/temporarily occupied premises caused by insured's negligence
  • Aggregates restore at policy anniversary
