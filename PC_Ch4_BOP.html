BOP Section II — business liability

Key difference from CGL
BOP liability is included in every policy — it cannot be written on a monoline (standalone) basis.
  • Usually written with an occurrence trigger
  • Covers BI, PD, and personal & advertising injury within coverage territory and policy period
  • Supplementary payments paid in addition to the limit of insurance
  • Insurer's duty to defend ends when limits are exhausted
Medical Expenses — BOP
  • Pays medical, dental, hospital, and funeral services
  • Must be incurred within 1 year from date of accident
  • Injury by accident on or next to insured's premises, or due to insured's operations
  • No fault required — similar to CGL's Coverage C

BOP exclusions — differences from CGL

Most BOP exclusions mirror the CGL. These are the notable differences:

Watercraft Exclusion
CGL exception
Nonowned watercraft less than 26 feet
BOP exception
Nonowned watercraft less than 51 feet — more generous
BOP-Only Exclusions (not in CGL)
Professional Services
No BI, PD, or personal/advertising injury from rendering or failing to render professional services
Criminal Acts
Explicitly excluded in BOP — intentional criminal acts of the insured
Employer's Liability
No BI to employee, or their spouse/children/siblings during employment. Exception: liability assumed in an insured contract.
Nuclear Energy
Nuclear energy liability exclusion applies to all liability coverages

Who is an insured — BOP

Individual / Sole Proprietor
Named insured + spouse — for business conduct only
Partnership / Joint Venture
Named insured, members, partners + spouses — for business conduct
LLC
Named insured + members. Managers insured for duties as managers
Corporation / Other Org
Named insured + executive officers/directors (duties) + stockholders (liability as stockholders)
Trust
Named insured + trustees for duties as trustees
Also Insureds
  • Volunteers performing duties related to the insured business
  • Employees in the course of employment
  • Any person (not employee/volunteer) acting as real estate manager
  • Person/org with temporary custody of property if named insured dies
  • Legal representative if named insured dies — for duties as such
No person/org insured for conduct of any current or past partnership, JV, or LLC not shown as named insured in declarations.

BOP limits of insurance

Unlike CGL (which states dollar aggregate limits), BOP aggregates are defined as multiples of the occurrence limit:

OCCURRENCE LIMIT
Listed on declarations — maximum per occurrence regardless of claimants
GENERAL AGGREGATE
= 2× the occurrence limit — covers BI, PD, personal & advertising injury, and medical expenses (non-products)
PRODUCTS / COMP OPS
= 2× the occurrence limit — separate aggregate for products and completed operations
FIRE DAMAGE LEGAL
Separate limit for fire damage to rented/temp-occupied premises — can be included in either aggregate based on claim type
  • Medical expenses paid per person, subject to occurrence limit
  • Limits apply separately to each consecutive annual period and any remaining period under 12 months

BOP general conditions — liability

Bankruptcy
Bankruptcy or insolvency does not relieve the insurer of any obligation
Duties After Loss
Promptly notify insurer; provide written notice of claims; notify of legal papers received; cooperate in investigation
Legal Action vs. Insurer
No party can join insurer in suit against insured. Must comply with all policy terms before suing. Settlement cannot exceed limits or include uncovered items.
Separation of Insureds
Limit paid once per occurrence regardless of number of claimants or insureds — does not increase limits
BOP Definitions to Know
Insured Product
Goods/products manufactured, sold, handled, or distributed by the insured or others trading under the insured's name. Includes containers, materials, parts, equipment.
Insured Contract
Lease of premises · sidetrack agreement · easement/license agreement · municipal indemnification · elevator maintenance · tort liability assumed in a contract pertaining to insured business.

Section III — cancellation notice periods

Insured can cancel anytime by written notice. Insurer must give advance notice — required timing depends on the reason:

5days
Special building conditions apply: Vacant/unoccupied 60+ consecutive days · Permanent repairs not contracted within 30 days of initial payment · Outstanding vacate/demolition order · Fixed/salvageable items removed · No utilities for 30+ days · Property taxes unpaid for 1+ year
10days
Nonpayment of premium
30days
Any other reason (may vary by state)
Important: Insurer only has to prove the notice was mailed to the first named insured — not that it was received. Return premium refund goes to first named insured.

Section III — other common conditions

Changes
Policy = entire contract. Changes only by written request of first named insured + insurer approval
Concealment / Fraud
Policy void if any insured commits fraud or intentionally conceals/misrepresents a material fact about policy, property, interest, or claim
Exam of Books
Insurer may examine books/records during policy period and up to 3 years after policy ends
Inspections
Insurer may inspect premises/operations at any reasonable time — for insurability, rating, and loss control. Does not guarantee safety or compliance.
Liberalization
If insurer adopts a revision that broadens coverage without additional premium within 45 days prior to or during the policy period → applies immediately
Other Insurance
If loss is covered by other insurance → insurer pays only the excess over the other coverage (regardless of collectibility)
Premiums
First named insured is responsible for paying premium; receives any refunds
Subrogation
Insurer has right to recover claim payment from negligent third parties
Transfer of Rights
Cannot transfer rights/duties without written consent of insurer. Upon death → automatically transferred to legal representative
