Crime Insurance

Discovery vs. Loss Sustained · Definitions · Burglary vs. Robbery vs. Theft · General Exclusions · Employee Theft · Forgery & Alteration

Crime program — overview

  • Can be added to a commercial package policy or written monoline
  • Fills gaps left by building and business personal property forms for crime losses
  • Each coverage selected has its own insuring agreement, conditions, and exclusions
  • Activated by the declarations page
Commercial Entities
Forms for private businesses
Government Entities
Forms for public/government entities
Coverage Triggers — Similar to Liability
Loss Sustained Form
Similar to occurrence — covers losses that occur and are discovered during the policy period.
Discovery Form
Similar to claims-made — covers losses discovered during the policy period, regardless of when they occurred.

Key definitions — Part 1

Employee
  • Natural person in insured's service + 30 days after termination
  • Temporary employee hired from an employment contractor
  • Managers, directors, trustees performing acts within usual employee duties
  • Former employees, managers, partners, directors, trustees working as consultants
Discover / Discovered
The time the insured first becomes aware of facts that would lead a reasonable person to assume a loss occurred.
Occurrence
An act or a series of acts — may or may not involve multiple people.
Premises
The interior of the premises where the insured conducts business.
Banking Premises
The interior of premises occupied by a banking institution or similar facility.

Key definitions — Part 2 (property types)

Money
Currency, coin, bank notes in current use, traveler's checks, register checks, money orders held for sale to the public
Securities
Negotiable and non-negotiable instruments or contracts representing money or property — tokens, tickets, revenue stamps, evidence of debt from credit/charge cards not issued by the insured
Funds
Money + Securities
Other Property
Property other than money or securities
Tangible Property
Any other property that has intrinsic value
Memory tip
Funds = Money + Securities
Other Property = everything else · Tangible Property = intrinsic value

Crime definitions — burglary, robbery, theft

🔓
Burglary
Forced entry into or out of premises with felonious intent. Policy requires visible signs of forced entry or exit following a loss.
🔒
Safe Burglary
Unlawful taking of covered property from within a locked safe or vault inside premises, or taking the entire safe/vault from the premises.
🔫
Robbery
Taking property from the care and custody of a person by causing or threatening bodily harm, or committing an obviously unlawful act witnessed by that person. Requires a victim present.
🕵️
Theft
Any act of stealing — encompasses both burglary and robbery. Broadest term.
❓
Mysterious Disappearance
Property gone with no apparent explanation. Considered the probability of theft. Most property policies deny claims for mysterious disappearance.

General exclusions — all crime forms

Acts by Insured/Partners/Members
No coverage for acts committed by the insured, partners, members, or acts by managers/directors/employees known to the insured before the policy period
Acts of Employees
Employee dishonest acts learned of before the policy period — excluded for all coverages except Employee Theft
Confidential Information
Unauthorized disclosure of patents, trade secrets, or customer lists
Data Security Breach
Fees, fines, penalties, and costs related to access or disclosure of another's personal/confidential information
Government Action
Seizure or destruction of property by governmental authority
Indirect Losses
Expenses incurred while establishing the amount of loss
Legal Fees
Legal expenses related to legal action
Nuclear / Pollution / War
Standard exclusions across all coverages

Employee theft (employee dishonesty)

What it covers
Loss of money, securities, and other property resulting from theft by an employee. Only coverage that overrides the general exclusion for employee acts.
Special Conditions
  • Coverage extension for employees temporarily outside the coverage territory for up to 90 days
  • Coverage is automatically cancelled on any employee when the insured discovers that employee has committed a dishonest act — whether or not the act occurred before employment
Additional Exclusions
Losses provable only through inventory shortage or profit/loss accounting
Losses from trading
Fraudulent signing, issuing, cancelling, or failing to cancel a warehouse receipt

Forgery or alteration

What it covers
Worldwide coverage for loss of money resulting from forgery and alteration of outgoing checks, drafts, or promissory notes. Also provides defense coverage if the insured is sued for failure to honor a financial instrument suspected of forgery.
Special Conditions
Proof of Loss
Insured must provide the insurer with the actual altered or forged check/instrument
Territory
Anywhere in the world — this is broader than most coverages
Electronic Signatures
Treated the same as handwritten signatures
Key distinction — Outgoing only
Forgery or alteration covers outgoing instruments (checks the insured writes). Incoming check fraud would fall under a different coverage.
