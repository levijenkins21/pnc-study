Bonds

3 parties · Surety vs. Insurance · Fidelity bonds · Types of employee fidelity bonds · One-bite rule

The 3 parties to a bond

Principal
Also: Obligor
Makes the promise. Purchases the bond. Goes through underwriting. Responsible for reimbursing surety if bond pays.
→
Obligee
Also: Insured
The promise is made to them. Requires the bond. Bond is payable to them if principal defaults.
→
Surety
Also: Guarantor
The bonding company. Provides the financial backing (bond penalty). Pays obligee if principal defaults.
Example
Building owner (obligee) requires contractor (principal) to finish by a date. Contractor buys a bond from a surety. If contractor misses the deadline → surety pays the owner the bond amount. Then contractor must reimburse the surety.

Surety vs. insurance — key differences

Insurance
2-party agreement — insurer and insured
Pays losses without recourse against the insured
Expects losses to occur
Insured does not reimburse the insurer
Covers uncertainty of loss
Surety
3-party contract — principal, obligee, surety
Does not expect to pay losses — guarantees performance
If bond pays, principal must reimburse surety
Pays only up to the stated penalty (bond limit)
Guarantees a specific outcome
Key exam point
Surety bonds are not insurance in the traditional sense. The biggest difference: the principal (obligor) is responsible for reimbursing the surety for any amount paid — there is right of recourse against the principal.

Fidelity bonds — overview

Surety Bonds
Guarantee performance of an obligation
Fidelity Bonds
Guarantee honesty and trust — employee dishonesty coverage
What Fidelity Covers
Losses caused by theft, embezzlement, forgery, misappropriation, or any other dishonest or fraudulent act by employees
Key Features
  • Written with a stated limit of liability
  • Covers acts committed during the bond period
  • Usually written with no expiration date — stays in force until cancelled
  • Discovery period: losses covered if discovered within 1 year after bond terminates (or 2 years for blanket position)
One-Bite Rule
The moment an employer becomes aware of an employee's dishonesty that could lead to a claim → coverage for that employee terminates immediately. Applies even if the act occurred before the current bond period or with a different employer.

Types of employee fidelity bonds

1
Individual
Covers 1 specifically named employee
2
Name Schedule
Covers several named employees for varying amounts — each listed by name on the bond
3
Position Schedule
Covers anyone occupying a scheduled position — listed by job title, not name. Coverage follows the position, not the person.
4
Commercial Blanket
Covers all employees. Limit of liability on a per loss basis — no matter how many employees were involved in one loss.
5
Blanket Position
Covers all employees. Limit of liability on a per employee basis — regardless of how many employees were involved. Discovery period is 2 years (vs. 1 year for others).
Commercial Blanket vs. Blanket Position
Both cover all employees — the difference is the limit basis. Blanket = per loss (1 payout for any loss regardless of # of employees involved). Blanket Position = per employee (each dishonest employee is its own limit).
